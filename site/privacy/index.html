Privacy policy

Last updated 10 October 2026

flarehand is a free, open-source plugin made by FlareWare Solutions. It runs inside the AI tool you already use. This page says what it stores, what it sends, and what we receive. We receive nothing.

The short version

  • flarehand has no server, no account, no telemetry and no analytics. It sends nothing to FlareWare Solutions.
  • It keeps a private knowledge base on your computer, and only after you say yes.
  • Its scripts go online only in the few cases listed below, and never send your notes or drafts.
  • Before text leaves your computer, it checks for credentials and personal data, and lets you decide.
  • You can delete everything it keeps, at any time.

What we receive

Nothing. FlareWare Solutions runs no service for flarehand. The plugin sends us no data, so we cannot see your knowledge base, your drafts or how you use it.

Your AI tool is separate

flarehand runs inside an agent such as Claude Code, Codex, Copilot, Cursor or Gemini CLI. That tool sends your conversation, and whatever the agent reads, to its model provider under its own terms. flarehand does not change that.

It only decides what the agent reads from your knowledge base. It reads only what the current request needs.

What flarehand stores, and where

  • Your knowledge base. A folder of plain text notes at ~/.flareware/flarehand, or %USERPROFILE%\.flareware\flarehand on Windows. flarehand creates it only after you agree. After that, it saves only what you agree to, usually the items you pick from the menu at the end of each job. It keeps a local history and never uploads or syncs anything.
  • What the knowledge base can hold. Your name, your role in your own words, and your settings. Also the notes, saved answers and copies of sources you chose to keep. That can include personal data, such as notes about colleagues, when you choose to save it.
  • Session files. Copies of the sources read in a session, and a checkpoint saved before your tool shortens a long conversation. They go to your system's temporary folder. flarehand deletes them after the session, or after 7 days at most.
  • Team playbooks. Shared templates and rules in a .flarehand folder in your repository, written only when you ask. They never hold notes or anything about a person.

When it goes online

flarehand's scripts make a network request only in these cases:

  • To fetch a web page that you or a tool named, when the agent uses it as a source.
  • To check again whether a source you pinned has changed.
  • When you ask, to check whether the links in a draft still work, or whether the Internet Archive has a copy of a dead link.
  • When you ask, to test whether your computer can reach the web.

Each request goes to the site in question, to archive.org for the Internet Archive check, or to example.com for the web test. It carries the web address and standard request headers. It never carries your notes, your drafts or your settings. The hooks and every other script make no network request.

Personal data

  • It may read personal data in your work, such as names and email addresses, because your work contains them.
  • It stores personal data only in your knowledge base, on your computer, and only after your yes.
  • It asks once before it keeps a password, an id number or someone's health details. It tells you each time it applies your answer.
  • By default, notes about people record what someone did, not their character. When someone is away, they keep the dates, not the reason.
  • Before text leaves your computer, it scans for credentials, id and card numbers, email addresses, phone numbers and health words. It shows what it found and waits for your decision.

Deleting your data

Everything flarehand keeps is on your computer, so you control it.

  • To remove one item, ask flarehand to forget it.
  • To remove everything, uninstall the plugin and delete the ~/.flareware/flarehand folder. Session files clear themselves within 7 days.
  • Uninstalling the plugin alone does not delete your knowledge base.

The exact commands are in Privacy and data, which also gives the technical detail behind this page.
