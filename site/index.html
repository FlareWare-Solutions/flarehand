The problem

AI assistants are eager to please. That is the problem.

Give one a vague request and it may guess. It can fill gaps with numbers and links that look right. It can take the first theory it hears as fact. By tomorrow, it has forgotten all of it.

How it works

What happens when you ask

The same five steps run every time, whether you ask for a postmortem, a customer reply or a plan.

  1. It names the job

    Before any work, it says what you need in plain words. If two readings are close, it shows you both.

    Sounds like you need a postmortem.

  2. It looks things up first

    Your files, your notes and the sources that fit your role come before any question. Finding facts is its job, not yours.

    Searched your notes, the repository and its history.

  3. It asks only what matters

    None for a quick lookup. A few for bigger work, each with a suggested answer. Say “just go” to accept them all.

    2. Who reads this? (your team)

  4. It shows where every fact came from

    Each specific gets a label. Then a check runs on the draft before you see it.

    Rolled back at 22:15 [your input]

  5. It keeps only what you approve

    Each job ends with a short menu. It saves nothing until you reply with the numbers you want.

    Keep any of this? Nothing is saved until you reply with numbers, or “none”.

Memory and privacy

It remembers what you approve. Nothing else.

flarehand keeps a private knowledge base on your computer. It is a folder of plain notes you can open, read and delete.

  • You decide what stays

    A yes covers only the lines you pick. Each save shows a one-line undo, and “forget” takes anything back.

  • Same question, same answer

    An answer you approved comes back word for word. If its source has changed, it checks again first.

  • It learns your way, and asks first

    Make the same edit twice and it offers to keep it as your preference. You answer mine, team, later or never.

  • No account, no tracking

    flarehand has no server and collects no usage data. Your AI tool still talks to its own provider, as it always does.

  • A check before anything leaves

    Before you send, it looks for passwords, personal data and client names. It shows what it found and lets you decide.

Questions

Short answers

Is it free?

Yes. flarehand is open source under the MIT license. There is no account and nothing to pay.

Do I need to be technical?

No. If you can type a request to your AI tool, you can use flarehand. Installing takes one or two lines, shown above. After that, you just ask for work the way you always do.

Does it send my data anywhere?

flarehand has no server and no telemetry, and your knowledge base never leaves your computer. Its scripts go online only to fetch or re-check a source you cite, or to test a link you asked about. Your AI tool still sends your conversation to its own provider, as it does without flarehand.

Does it save anything without asking?

No. Nothing goes into your knowledge base without a yes. Every save shows a one-line undo, and you can delete the whole folder at any time.

Can my team share templates and rules?

Yes. A .flarehand folder in your team's repository holds shared templates, a glossary and house rules. It never holds notes or anything about a person.
